Moving to UAE as a South African

✓ SA-verified facts Updated August 2026 15 min read

There are an estimated 45,000–50,000 South Africans living in the UAE — and for good reason. Zero income tax, world-class infrastructure, short flight home (8 hours Joburg–Dubai), a large SA community and a strong job market. Here's everything you need to know before you board that Emirates flight.

🇿🇦 Good news for South Africans SA passport holders receive a 30-day visa on arrival in the UAE, making initial visits or job-seeking trips easy. You don't need to apply for a tourist visa in advance.

Visa Options for South Africans

The type of visa you get determines everything: what you can do, how long you can stay and how you get your Emirates ID. Here are the main options:

Employment Visa (most common)

Your employer sponsors your visa and residence. They handle most of the process. You'll get a 2- or 3-year renewable residence permit. Requirements vary by industry, but typically require a certified copy of your SA degree and attested qualifications.

Freelance Visa

You can work as a self-employed individual without an employer or a company. Dubai DED, Fujairah Creative City and Masdar City free zone all offer freelance permits from AED 7,500–15,000/year. You'll get a residence visa tied to your permit.

Investor Visa (2 years)

Requires an investment of at least AED 75,000 in a UAE business or AED 750,000 in real estate. Gives you a 2-year renewable residence visa and freedom to do business.

Golden Visa (10 years)

UAE's long-term residency option. Available to investors (AED 2M+), outstanding professionals (doctors, scientists, engineers, executives), entrepreneurs with funded startups, and talented individuals in creative fields. No sponsor required — you are self-sponsored.

💡 Which visa do most South Africans get? The vast majority get an employment visa through their employer. If you're moving without a job, a tourist visa on arrival gives you 30 days to look — many extend this once.

Before You Leave South Africa

Sort these documents before you board. Some take weeks to process and are hard to do remotely:

1

Valid SA Passport

Must have at least 6 months validity. Renew at Home Affairs or a South African embassy/consulate. Allow 4–8 weeks.

2

Attested Qualifications

Your SA university degree needs to be authenticated by SAQA (South African Qualifications Authority) and then attested by the UAE Embassy in Pretoria. Budget 4–6 weeks and ±R2,500.

3

Police Clearance Certificate

Required for your UAE residence visa. Apply at SAPS or online via the eDHA portal. Takes 2–6 weeks. Must be apostilled if requested.

4

Unabridged Birth Certificate

Required if you're bringing children. Apply at Home Affairs. Allow 6–10 weeks — start early. Children with different surnames from either parent will need both parents' passports.

5

SARS Tax Affairs

Make sure you're tax compliant before you leave. Get a tax clearance certificate if required. Consider whether you need to complete a financial emigration (now called "Cease to be a tax resident"). See our Money & Banking guide.

What to Pack (and What to Buy in UAE)

Bring from South Africa

  • Your SA driving licence — you'll convert it to a UAE licence (much cheaper than sitting for a UAE test if you have an SA licence)
  • SA products you can't live without — Ouma rusks, Mrs Ball's chutney, biltong spices, Simba chips. Stock up, they're hard to find (SPAR has some, but limited)
  • Braai tongs and SA recipes — UAE has meat, you bring the culture
  • All your original documents — degree, police clearance, birth certificates
  • Professional clothing — the weather is hot but offices are cold (AC)

Buy in UAE (don't waste suitcase space)

  • Electronics (iPhone, laptop — often cheaper and newer stock)
  • Household goods and furniture (IKEA, Home Centre, PAN Emirates)
  • Spices and halal meat (incredible variety at any supermarket)
  • Gym gear (cheaper than SA)

The 10 Things Every Saffa Wishes They Knew First

  1. Summers are brutal. June–September temperatures reach 45°C+ with humidity. You will live indoors with AC. Plan your move for October–April if possible.
  2. Cash is still used everywhere, but cards are accepted almost anywhere. Carry AED 200–500 cash for taxis, markets and smaller shops.
  3. Driving is aggressive. Get used to tailgating, lane-changing and speeding. Careem/Uber are good alternatives until you're comfortable.
  4. Alcohol is available but regulated. You can drink at licensed venues (hotels, restaurants, bars). Buying from a liquor store requires a personal liquor licence (apply at MMI or African + Eastern).
  5. Friday and Saturday is the weekend, not Saturday and Sunday. Government and many businesses are closed Friday mornings.
  6. Ramadan changes everything. Eating, drinking and smoking in public during daylight hours is illegal during Ramadan. Restaurants are open but often curtained. Hours shift significantly.
  7. VPNs are in a grey area. Using a VPN for legitimate purposes is generally fine; using it for illegal content (VoIP calls without a licence) is not.
  8. WhatsApp calling works, but standard voice calls over some other apps (Skype, FaceTime audio) may not. Use WhatsApp or a local SIM for calls.
  9. Your SA driving record means nothing here. You'll need to convert your SA licence (much cheaper than a test) within 12 months of getting your residence visa.
  10. The SA community is your greatest asset. Join the Saffa.ae WhatsApp groups and SABCO within your first week. The community will save you hours of research.

Cost of Living — Quick Reference

💰 Rough AED monthly costs (Dubai, mid-range)
  • Studio apartment: AED 4,000–6,500/month
  • 1-bed apartment: AED 6,000–10,000/month
  • 3-bed villa: AED 15,000–30,000+/month
  • Groceries (family of 2): AED 2,000–3,000/month
  • Health insurance (basic): AED 500–1,500/month
  • School fees: AED 2,500–6,000/month per child
  • Car (finance, mid-range): AED 1,500–2,500/month
