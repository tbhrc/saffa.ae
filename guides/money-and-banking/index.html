Money & Banking Guide

✓ SA-verified facts Updated August 2026 12 min read

Living in the UAE with South African financial ties creates a unique set of challenges and opportunities. Zero UAE income tax means you keep more of your salary, but SARS still wants to know about your worldwide income. Here's everything you need to manage your money smart as a Saffa in the UAE.

Opening a UAE Bank Account

You need a UAE bank account to receive your salary, pay rent and manage daily expenses. Here are the banks most popular with SA expats:

Emirates NBD (most popular with SA expats)

The largest UAE bank by assets. Excellent app, good English service, widely accepted everywhere. The Go4it account (salary transfer account) has no monthly fee if your salary is deposited. Mashreq Neo and ENBD digital accounts allow instant online opening.

First Abu Dhabi Bank (FAB)

Largest bank by market cap in MENA. Good for higher-income professionals. Better interest rates on savings. Abu Dhabi-headquartered but operates nationally.

Mashreq Bank

Excellent digital banking through Mashreq Neo. Known for good customer service and fast account opening. Popular with freelancers and entrepreneurs.

ADCB (Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank)

Great for Abu Dhabi-based Saffas. Strong mortgage product for those buying property.

📋 Documents needed to open a UAE bank account:
  • Original SA passport (valid)
  • Emirates ID (original)
  • Residence visa page (copy)
  • Salary certificate or employment letter (for salary accounts)
  • 3 months bank statements from your SA bank
  • Proof of UAE address (DEWA bill, lease agreement)

SARS Tax for South Africans Abroad

South Africa has a residence-based tax system. This means SA tax residents are taxed on worldwide income — including income earned in the UAE. Understanding your obligations is critical.

The Foreign Income Exemption (Section 10(1)(o)(ii))

If you spend 183 days or more outside South Africa in a 12-month period (with at least 60 of those being consecutive days), your foreign employment income may be exempt from SA income tax up to a threshold.

✓ 2025/2026 Tax Year Exemption Threshold The first R1.25 million of qualifying foreign employment income is exempt from SA income tax under Section 10(1)(o)(ii). Income above this threshold is taxed by SARS at your marginal SA rate (up to 45%). This means many mid-salary UAE earners effectively pay no SA tax.

Do you need to file a SA tax return from UAE?

Most South Africans living in UAE are still SA tax residents and must file an annual SARS return even if they owe nothing. Exceptions apply only if you have formally ceased tax residency (financial emigration).

⚠️ Don't ignore SARS while abroad SARS has increased enforcement on non-resident compliance since 2023. Saffas who ignored SA returns while in UAE have received unexpected tax bills, interest and penalties years later. File every year, even if it's a nil return.

Financial Emigration (Ceasing SA Tax Residency)

Financial emigration (now officially called "Ceasing to be a Tax Resident") means formally notifying SARS that you are no longer a South African tax resident. This is a significant and largely permanent decision.

What financial emigration means:

  • You stop being taxed by SARS on foreign income — but any SA-sourced income (rentals, dividends, interest) is still subject to SA withholding tax
  • You're treated as having disposed of your SA assets at market value on the day you cease residency (the "exit charge") — capital gains tax may apply
  • Your SA retirement funds (provident funds, pension funds, RAs) can be accessed after 3 years of non-tax-residency — but there are tax implications
  • SARB exchange control changes — you may move assets out of SA more freely, but still within limits
⚠️ Financial emigration is NOT casual Get advice from a qualified SA tax advisor before proceeding. The exit tax charge can be significant for those with property, shares or retirement savings. See our directory for SA tax advisors in UAE.

Sending Money Between ZAR and AED

One of the most common needs for Saffas in UAE — sending money home to SA or bringing SA funds to UAE.

Best options (2026):

  • Wise (formerly TransferWise) — Best exchange rates, low transparent fees, fast. App-based. Can hold both AED and ZAR. Most popular with Saffas for regular transfers.
  • Instarem — Good rates, especially for larger amounts. UAE-licensed.
  • Western Union / MoneyGram — Available but poorer rates. Use for emergencies or cash pickup only.
  • UAE bank international transfers (SWIFT) — Expensive fees (AED 50–150 per transfer) and poor rates. Avoid for regular transfers.
  • SARB Single Discretionary Allowance — SA residents can transfer up to R1 million/year to a SA resident without SARB approval. As a non-resident, your limits differ — consult a forex advisor for large amounts.
💡 ZAR/AED Rate Context As of mid-2026, the rand trades at approximately ZAR 19–21 to AED 1. UAE salaries in AED translate to significant SA spending power — a R30,000/month SA salary earns AED 1,500–1,600, while a mid-level UAE professional earning AED 20,000/month effectively earns R380,000–400,000/month in SA terms.

SA Retirement Savings from UAE

  • Retirement Annuities (RAs) — You can continue contributing to an SA RA while abroad. Contributions are deductible against SA taxable income (useful if you still earn SA-sourced income). You can only access RA savings at age 55+.
  • Preservation Funds — If you left SA employment, your provident/pension fund can move into a preservation fund. No tax while inside the fund. Better than withdrawing and taking a tax hit.
  • New offshore investments — UAE has no capital gains tax, making it attractive for offshore investing. Speak to a SA-registered financial advisor who understands both SA and UAE products.
